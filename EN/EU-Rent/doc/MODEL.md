# EU-Rent — a CaseTalk information model

An FCO-IM information model of **EU-Rent**, the fictitious car rental company that has become
the standard worked example for business vocabulary and business rules.

Built in CaseTalk from the published EU-Rent material — see [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

> **These files are project members.** Each was attached with the MCP `markdown_file`
> tool (`operation: register`), so they appear in the CaseTalk project window under the
> repository node and survive a project save. All except SESSION-NOTES.md are also flagged
> `in_report`, so they are pulled into the generated project (MIM) report — the project window

> save sweeps *unregistered* files out of the project folder into `unused files.zip`, and

## The business, in one paragraph

EU-Rent operates in several countries. In each country it has local areas, and each local area
contains branches. It rents cars to customers from branches, and one-way rentals — picked up
at one branch, dropped off at another — are allowed. Each car is of a given car model; car
models are grouped into car groups, and **all cars in a car group have the same rental
tariff**. Cars are serviced at 5000 mile intervals. EU-Rent records "bad experiences" with
drivers — police actions, unpaid fines, damage, late or non-return — and may bar the drivers
who cause them.

## How the model is organised

Six containers hold the subject areas:

- **EU-Rent Network** — Country, Local Area, Branch
- **EU-Rent Fleet** — Car Group, Car Model, Car, purchase date, condition, odometer, service
- **EU-Rent Parties** — Person and its subtypes Customer and Driver, licences, bad experiences, barring
- **EU-Rent Rental Process** — Rental and its derived subtypes Advance, Walk-in, One-Way and Open Rental
- **EU-Rent Charging** — the tariff per car group per country, and the charges a rental incurs
- **EU-Rent Business Rules** — R01–R20, R22, R23 (R21 reserved)

Documentation lives in the paragraph chapter **EU-Rent Case Study**: business context, scope,
how to read the model, modelling decisions, sources.

Annotations are uniform. Every rule carries a `[Definition]` (its SBVR-style statement plus
what enforces it) and a `[Source]`. Every core type carries a `[Definition]`, most carry an
`[Example]`, and the ones whose shape comes straight from the case carry a `[Source]` with the
URL it was taken from.

## Five modelling decisions worth knowing

1. **Renter and driver are separated.** Person is the supertype; Customer and Driver are
   subtypes. A company may rent a car it never drives, and a driver may drive on someone
   else's contract. A totality constraint spanning both subtype roles says every person must
   be at least one of the two.
2. **The tariff hangs off Car Group and Country** — never off Car or Car Model. That is what
   makes "all cars in a car group have the same tariff" true *by construction* rather than by
   rule (R17).
3. **Life cycles are state-machine value constraints.** `Rental Status` and `Condition` each
   carry their legal transitions, so what may follow what is visible in one place instead of
   being scattered over date facts.
4. **Four rental subtypes are derived, not asserted.** One-Way, Advance, Walk-in and Open
   Rental follow from comparing branches, dates or state, so they carry no facts of their own.

5. **Every distance is in miles.** The SBVR-era sources give the service interval as 5000
   miles (R14); the original 1995 case study gives the disposal limit as 40,000 kilometres
   (R21). Rather than carry two units, the model states the disposal limit as 25,000 miles
   and says so in the rule's annotation and in `Mileage`'s definition.

## State of the model

Third pass, 2026-09-08: the fleet disposal rule of the original case study is in as **R21**,
with a `Purchase Date of Car` fact type (UC + TC on Car), a `Sold` state in the `Condition`
state machine, and three more cars in the population — two counter-examples (one over age,
one over distance, both still Available) and one car sold at one year old. The four earlier
cars now sit under the limit. The well-formedness check still reports **0 errors**.

Second pass, 2026-09-05. The well-formedness check reports **0 errors**. What changed since
the first pass:

- **Identification repaired.** The eight object types are identified by their own object
  type expression (`Country` = `<Country Code>`, `Car Model` = `<Manufacturer> <Model Name>`,
  ...). The eight "X Identification" fact types and the eight generated "X ID" labels are gone.
- **Population is in.** Every fact type carries its sample tuples, plus the counter-examples
  from POPULATION.md flagged as such.
- **Six glossary terms are Concepts again** — EU-Rent, Authorized Area, Car Group Upgrade,
  Car Transfer, Rental Agreement, Scheduled Service — placed in their containers, with their
  definitions and sources. The six accidental link fact types are gone.
- **Every behavioural rule carries a `[Derivation]`.** RULES.md marks which rules are
  structural and which are derivations; the four derived Rental subtypes are flagged derivable.
- **The composite fact types** (Car Service, Car Transfer Movement, Rental Charge, Rental
  Period, Rental Tariff) are flagged composite, so the normalisation rule leaves them alone.

Still open, on the project todo list (which CaseTalk renders into `README.md`):

- **The expressions are unconfirmed.** The wordings come from published sources, not from an
  interview with a domain expert, so they are deliberately left unconfirmed.
- **Three thresholds are assumed** (R06 age 21, R07 90 days, R19 three bad experiences).
- **Eight unused wordy object type expressions** (`country <Country Code>` and the like) are
  left over from the repair and want deleting — a consent click in CaseTalk.
- **The derivations are prose**, not an executable notation.
- **The counter-example rentals** carry only the fact they violate.

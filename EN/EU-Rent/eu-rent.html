EU-Rent Case Study

1. Business Context

EU-Rent is a fictitious car rental company that has become the standard worked example for business vocabulary and business rules. It operates in several countries. In each country it has local areas, and each local area contains branches. EU-Rent rents cars to customers from branches, and one-way rentals - picked up at one branch and dropped off at another - are allowed. Each car is of a given car model; car models are grouped into car groups, and all cars in a car group carry the same rental tariff. Cars are serviced at 5000 mile intervals. EU-Rent records "bad experiences" with drivers - police actions, unpaid fines, damage, late or non-return - and may bar the drivers who cause them.

2. Scope Of This Model

This information model covers the five subject areas held in the containers of this repository: the Network (Country, Local Area, Branch), the Fleet (Car Group, Car Model, Car, condition, odometer, service), the Parties (Person with its Customer and Driver subtypes, licences, bad experiences, barring), the Rental Process (the rental, its period, its allocated car, its drivers and its state) and Charging (the tariff per car group per country, and the charges a rental incurs). Out of scope: reservations inventory and availability forecasting, accident and insurance claim handling, invoicing and payment settlement, and the loyalty scheme that later versions of the case add.

3. How To Read This Model

The model is built fact-first, in FCO-IM style: every fact type carries one expression in natural language, and the object and label types emerge from the roles in those expressions. Identification is stated with uniqueness constraints, not with keys: an object type is identified by the roles it plays under a UC. Car Model shows a composite identification - the UC spans manufacturer AND model name together. Mandatory participation is stated with totality constraints. Enumerations and life cycles are stated with value constraints: Car Group Code is a labelled enumeration, while Condition and Rental Status are state machines whose transitions say which state may follow which. The twenty business rules R01-R20 live as separate rule types; each says in its [Definition] whether the model already enforces it structurally or whether it needs a derivation expression.

4. Modelling Decisions

Five decisions are worth knowing. First, renter and driver are separated: Person is the supertype and Customer and Driver are subtypes, because a company may rent a car it never drives, and a driver may drive on someone else's contract. Second, the tariff hangs off Car Group and Country, never off Car or Car Model - that is what makes "all cars in a car group have the same tariff" true by construction rather than by rule. Third, the rental life cycle is carried by a state-machine value constraint rather than by separate date facts, so the legal transitions are visible in one place. Fourth, One-Way Rental, Advance Rental, Walk-in Rental and Open Rental are subtypes of Rental that are DERIVED - they follow from comparing branches, dates or state - and so they carry no facts of their own. Fifth, every distance in the model is in miles: the SBVR-era sources give the service interval as 5000 miles (R14), while the original 1995 case study gives the disposal limit as 40,000 kilometres (R21); rather than carry two units, the model states the disposal limit as 25,000 miles and says so in the rule.

5. Sources

The oldest source of the case is the GUIDE Business Rules Project final report "Defining Business Rules ~ What Are They Really?" by the Business Rules Group (first published as a GUIDE publication in 1995, revised 1997, revision 1.3 July 2000; https://www.businessrulesgroup.org/first_paper/BRG-whatisBR_3ed.pdf ). Its Appendix D, "Case Study: EU-Rent Car Rentals", was developed by Model Systems Ltd. of the United Kingdom (example by John Hall) and may be copied freely if its source is acknowledged. That original describes EU-Rent as one of three businesses of EU-Corporation (with EU-Fly and EU-Stay), with 1000 branches, service depots serving several branches each, cars sold at one year old or 40,000 km, and the rules that a customer has only one car on rental at a time and that the signing driver must not currently have an EU-Rent car out. The case later became the annex of the OMG SBVR specification. The material this model was actually built from is the SBVR-era retelling: the EU-Rent example page at https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/EU-Rent.html ; the SBVR introduction with the sample rules at https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/sbvr_intro_2.html ; the EU-Rent case description at https://caminao.blog/community/sample-problems/eu-rent-car/ ; and "SBVR Speaks on Rules: Violations of Business Rules" at https://www.brcommunity.com/articles.php?id=c009 , from which the Area of Usage Rule and its remedial rule are quoted verbatim. Where the sources leave a detail open - the exact bad-experience threshold, the minimum driver age, the 90-day cap - the model states a plausible value and says so in the rule's annotation.

Five modelling decisions worth knowing

  1. Renter and driver are separated. Person is the supertype; Customer and Driver are subtypes. A company may rent a car it never drives, and a driver may drive on someone else's contract. A totality constraint spanning both subtype roles says every person must be at least one of the two.

  2. The tariff hangs off Car Group and Country — never off Car or Car Model. That is what makes "all cars in a car group have the same tariff" true by construction rather than by rule (R17).

  3. Life cycles are state-machine value constraints. Rental Status and Condition each carry their legal transitions, so what may follow what is visible in one place instead of being scattered over date facts.

  4. Four rental subtypes are derived, not asserted. One-Way, Advance, Walk-in and Open Rental follow from comparing branches, dates or state, so they carry no facts of their own.

  5. Every distance is in miles. The SBVR-era sources give the service interval as 5000 miles (R14); the original 1995 case study gives the disposal limit as 40,000 kilometres (R21). Rather than carry two units, the model states the disposal limit as 25,000 miles and says so in the rule's annotation and in Mileage's definition.

EU-Rent — business rules

The twenty-three rules held as rule OFTypes in the container EU-Rent Business Rules. Each rule in the repository carries its statement in a [Definition] annotation and its provenance in a [Source] annotation; both are reproduced here.

Structural means the model already enforces the rule through a uniqueness, totality or value constraint — no code can violate it. Derivation means the rule cannot be a constraint on a single fact type; since 2026-09-05 each such rule carries a [Derivation] annotation in the repository that states it as a check over role paths (for example FOR EACH Rental R: COUNT(Additional Driver[R]) <= 3), names the counter-example in the population that violates it, and says which threshold is assumed. The four derived Rental subtypes (Advance, Walk-in, One-Way, Open) are flagged derivable with their defining condition. The notation is readable, not executable — choosing a formal target is on the todo list. This is the distinction SBVR draws between structural (definitional) rules and behavioural rules, and it is the honest way to read the model: half of EU-Rent's rules are simply not expressible as constraints on single fact types.

R21 is the fleet disposal rule of the original 1995 case study, modelled on 2026-09-08 with a Purchase Date of Car fact type (UC + TC on Car) and a Sold state in the Condition state machine, reachable from Available and In Service, never from Rented.

Each rule is linked to what it governs

Since 2026-09-08 every rule carries a link role to the fact types (and, where the rule is about the type itself, the object or label type) it constrains: R03 to Additional Driver, R14 to Car Odometer Reading, Car Service and Car Condition, R21 to Purchase Date of Car, Car Odometer Reading and Car Condition, and so on - 62 links over the 23 rules. The roles tool (partof=<rule>) lists them, and CaseTalk's relationship views draw them, so "which rules touch this fact type?" is a query rather than a search through the notes.

Quoted verbatim

Two rules are quoted word for word from the Business Rules Journal, where they are used to show the SBVR distinction between a behavioural rule and the remedial rule that responds when it is broken:

R10 — "No rented car of an open rental may be outside the authorized area of the rental."

R11 — "An open rental must be canceled if the Area of Usage Rule is violated by the rental."

— SBVR Speaks on Rules: Violations of Business Rules, https://www.brcommunity.com/articles.php?id=c009

R03 is likewise a standard SBVR EU-Rent example statement: "It is obligatory that each rental has at most three additional drivers."

R22 and R23 are quoted from the original 1995 case study (Business Rules Group report, Appendix D, by Model Systems Ltd.): "A customer can have several reservations but only one car rented at a time" and "The driver who signs the rental agreement must not currently have a EU-Rent car on rental." Rental IS the rental agreement in this model, and the signing driver is the main driver (R20).

R21 is quoted from the same appendix: "Cars are to be sold when they reach one year old or 40,000 kilometers, whichever occurs first." The odometer in this model is in miles, because the SBVR-era sources give the service interval that way (R14); the 40,000 km limit is applied as 25,000 miles (24,855 rounded) and the rule's [Definition] says so.

EU-Rent — sources

EU-Rent was originally developed by Model Systems Ltd. of the United Kingdom. It became the case study of the OMG SBVR specification (the EU-Rent annex), and is used throughout the work of the Business Rules Group. It is also a standing example in the Object-Role Modeling literature, which is where the branch / car group / blacklisted-customer framing comes from.

Every element in the repository carries its provenance in a [Source] annotation.

The original (oldest) source

Source What it is
Defining Business Rules ~ What Are They Really? — the Business Rules Group, final report of the GUIDE Business Rules Project. First published as a GUIDE publication in 1995, revised 1997, revision 1.3 July 2000 (the readable copy). Appendix D, Case Study: EU-Rent Car Rentals, "developed by Model Systems, Ltd. of the United Kingdom" (example by John Hall). The material "may be copied and used freely, if its source is clearly acknowledged". Every later retelling, including the SBVR annex, descends from it.

The original differs from the SBVR-era pages the model was built from in a few points, each now on the todo list:

  • EU-Rent is one of three businesses of EU-Corporation, with EU-Fly and EU-Stay hotels and a shared customer base.
  • It has 1000 branches, plus separate service depots that each serve several branches and take maintenance bookings subject to capacity.
  • Cars are sold at one year old or 40,000 km — modelled as R21 on 2026-09-08, with a Purchase Date of Car fact type and a Sold state. The 5000-mile service interval (R14) is from the later SBVR retelling, so the model keeps miles and applies the limit as 25,000 miles.
  • A customer may hold several reservations but only one car on rental at a time, and the signing driver must not currently have an EU-Rent car out — modelled as R22 and R23.

Where the model goes beyond the sources

Three thresholds are assumed, because the published material states the shape of the rule without a number: the minimum driver age of 21 (R06), the 90-day maximum rental period (R07), and the three bad experiences that bar a driver (R19). Each says so in its own annotation and each is a question on the project todo list.

Two structures are modelling choices, not statements of the case: the Person supertype with Customer and Driver subtypes (the sources treat renter and driver as separate roles but do not say how to type them), and the rental life cycle as a state machine (the sources speak of open, cancelled and closed rentals without giving a transition diagram).

The Business Rules Group's own EU-Rent material and the OMG SBVR annex contain considerably more rules than the twenty modelled here — the annex runs to roughly a hundred. The twenty were chosen to cover each kind of rule once: structural, behavioural, remedial, derivation, cardinality, and a join constraint.

EU-Rent — Organisational Capabilities

Derived from the conceptual model eu-rent.ig (117 object-fact types), organised around the model's six business containers and its 20 named business rules. This document describes what the organisation is able to do; the model describes the facts each capability produces and consumes.

Business Context

EU-Rent is a car rental company operating a network of branches across several countries. It rents cars from its fleet to customers under rental agreements, charges for those rentals, and enforces a body of business rules covering eligibility, availability, pricing and vehicle servicing.

Capability Map

EU-Rent's capabilities correspond one-to-one with the model's container structure. Each capability below lists the concepts it is built on and the business rules that govern it.

1. Network Management (EU-Rent Network)

Operate and structure the geographic branch network.

  • Manages: Country, Local Area, Branch, City
  • Enables: locating branches, defining service areas, supporting one-way rentals between branches (Pick Up / Drop Off Branch).

2. Fleet Management (EU-Rent Fleet)

Acquire, classify, position and maintain the vehicle fleet.

  • Manages: Car, Car Model, Car Group, Manufacturer, Home Branch, Car Transfer, Car Service, Odometer/Mileage.
  • Governed by:
    • R01 — a Car Model belongs to exactly one Car Group
    • R14 — service interval of 5,000 miles
    • R15 — only an available car may be rented

3. Party Management (EU-Rent Parties)

Manage the people and organisations EU-Rent deals with.

  • Manages: Person, Customer, Driver, Additional Driver, Driving Licence, Credit Card, Barred Driver, Bad Experience.
  • Governed by:
    • R04 — a barred driver may not drive
    • R05 — a driver must hold a valid licence
    • R06 — minimum driver age
    • R19 — three bad experiences bar a driver

4. Rental Process Management (EU-Rent Rental Process)

Handle the full rental lifecycle from request to return.

  • Manages: Rental (Advance, Walk-in, Open, One-Way), Rental Agreement, Rental Period, Rental State, Requested/Allocated Car Group, Main & Additional Drivers, Actual Return.
  • Governed by:
    • R02 — a rental requests exactly one car group
    • R03 — maximum three additional drivers
    • R07 — maximum rental duration
    • R08 — drop-off branch must be an EU-Rent branch
    • R10 / R11 — area-of-usage rule and its remedial rule
    • R12 — free upgrade when the requested group is unavailable
    • R13 — the allocated car matches or exceeds the requested group
    • R16 — rental guaranteed by credit card
    • R20 — the renter drives or nominates a driver

5. Charging & Revenue (EU-Rent Charging)

Price rentals and calculate charges.

  • Manages: Rental Tariff, Daily Rate, Rental Charge, Charge Kind/Amount, Advance Rental.
  • Governed by:
    • R09 — one-way rental incurs a location penalty charge
    • R17 — one tariff per car group per country
    • R18 — late return incurs a penalty

6. Governance & Business Rules (EU-Rent Business Rules)

Define, hold and enforce the 20 named business rules (R01–R20) that constrain all other capabilities. This is the organisation's rule-management capability — the single place where policy is expressed as verifiable constraints on the facts above.

Registration Number

Definition
The number plate of the car as issued by its country of registration, prefixed with the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code of that country and a hyphen, because a plate is unique only within the country that issued it: NL-G-482-TK, BE-2-XYZ-123, DE-D-AB-1234. The plate is written as issued. Dutch plates follow the series issued since 2019, one letter, three digits, two letters, without vowels; Belgian plates since 2010 are an index digit, three letters and three digits; German plates start with the district code (D for Dusseldorf), then one or two letters and up to four digits. Under R21 no fleet car is older than one year, so every plate belongs to the currently issued series of its country. The value pattern covers the three countries in the sample; another country needs the pattern extended.

R04 Barred Driver May Not Drive

Definition
It is prohibited that a barred driver is the main driver or an additional driver of a rental. A behavioural rule: it can be violated in the real world, which is exactly why EU-Rent records it.
Source

R21 Car Sold At One Year Or 40000 Km

Definition
It is obligatory that a car is sold when it reaches one year old or 40,000 kilometres, whichever comes first. A car whose purchase date lies more than one year in the past, or whose odometer reading exceeds 40,000 km (applied as 25,000 miles), must be in condition Sold. A car is sold from Available or from In Service, never while Rented, and a sold car never returns to the fleet - both are transitions of the Condition state machine. A behavioural rule: the fleet is reviewed against it, and the remedial action is the transition to Sold. It reads Purchase Date of Car and Car Odometer Reading.
Source
Verbatim from the original case study: "Cars are to be sold when they reach one year old or 40,000 kilometers, whichever occurs first." - Appendix D "Case Study: EU-Rent Car Rentals" (developed by Model Systems Ltd., example by John Hall) of the Business Rules Group / GUIDE Business Rules Project final report "Defining Business Rules ~ What Are They Really?" (1995; rev. 1.3 July 2000). https://www.businessrulesgroup.org/first_paper/BRG-whatisBR_3ed.pdf . Not in the SBVR-era retellings the rest of the model was built from; those give the service interval in miles (R14), so the model keeps one unit, miles, and applies 40,000 km as 25,000 miles (24,855 rounded).
Derivation
FOR EACH Car C WHERE Car Condition[C].Condition NOT IN ('Sold', 'Written Off'): (EvaluationDate - Purchase Date of Car[C].Purchase Date) < 1 year AND Car Odometer Reading[C].Mileage <= 25000 (40,000 km). Violated as soon as either bound is passed; the remedial action is Available -> Sold or In Service -> Sold, and a Rented car is sold on its return. A car without an odometer reading is judged on age alone. Age makes this rule time-dependent: the population is evaluated on 2026-09-08. Counter-examples in the population: NL-K-903-LP, purchased 2025-06-02, fifteen months old at 19,300 miles and still Available (age); DE-D-CD-5678, purchased 2026-01-12, eight months old at 31,200 miles (50,200 km) and still Available (distance). R22 One Car On Rental At A Time

Definition

EU-Rent Case Study

1. Business Context

EU-Rent is a fictitious car rental company that has become the standard worked example for business vocabulary and business rules. It operates in several countries. In each country it has local areas, and each local area contains branches. EU-Rent rents cars to customers from branches, and one-way rentals - picked up at one branch and dropped off at another - are allowed. Each car is of a given car model; car models are grouped into car groups, and all cars in a car group carry the same rental tariff. Cars are serviced at 5000 mile intervals. EU-Rent records "bad experiences" with drivers - police actions, unpaid fines, damage, late or non-return - and may bar the drivers who cause them.

2. Scope Of This Model

This information model covers the five subject areas held in the containers of this repository: the Network (Country, Local Area, Branch), the Fleet (Car Group, Car Model, Car, condition, odometer, service), the Parties (Person with its Customer and Driver subtypes, licences, bad experiences, barring), the Rental Process (the rental, its period, its allocated car, its drivers and its state) and Charging (the tariff per car group per country, and the charges a rental incurs). Out of scope: reservations inventory and availability forecasting, accident and insurance claim handling, invoicing and payment settlement, and the loyalty scheme that later versions of the case add.

3. How To Read This Model

The model is built fact-first, in FCO-IM style: every fact type carries one expression in natural language, and the object and label types emerge from the roles in those expressions. Identification is stated with uniqueness constraints, not with keys: an object type is identified by the roles it plays under a UC. Car Model shows a composite identification - the UC spans manufacturer AND model name together. Mandatory participation is stated with totality constraints. Enumerations and life cycles are stated with value constraints: Car Group Code is a labelled enumeration, while Condition and Rental Status are state machines whose transitions say which state may follow which. The twenty business rules R01-R20 live as separate rule types; each says in its [Definition] whether the model already enforces it structurally or whether it needs a derivation expression.

4. Modelling Decisions

Four decisions are worth knowing. First, renter and driver are separated: Person is the supertype and Customer and Driver are subtypes, because a company may rent a car it never drives, and a driver may drive on someone else's contract. Second, the tariff hangs off Car Group and Country, never off Car or Car Model - that is what makes "all cars in a car group have the same tariff" true by construction rather than by rule. Third, the rental life cycle is carried by a state-machine value constraint rather than by separate date facts, so the legal transitions are visible in one place. Fourth, One-Way Rental, Advance Rental, Walk-in Rental and Open Rental are subtypes of Rental that are DERIVED - they follow from comparing branches, dates or state - and so they carry no facts of their own.

5. Sources

EU-Rent was originally developed by Model Systems Ltd. and is the case study of the OMG SBVR specification (the EU-Rent annex), where it is also used by the Business Rules Group. The material this model was built from: the EU-Rent example page at https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/EU-Rent.html ; the SBVR introduction with the sample rules at https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/sbvr_intro_2.html ; the EU-Rent case description at https://caminao.blog/community/sample-problems/eu-rent-car/ ; and "SBVR Speaks on Rules: Violations of Business Rules" at https://www.brcommunity.com/articles.php?id=c009 , from which the Area of Usage Rule and its remedial rule are quoted verbatim. Where the sources leave a detail open - the exact bad-experience threshold, the minimum driver age, the 90-day cap - the model states a plausible value and says so in the rule's annotation.

Annotations are uniform. Every rule carries a [Definition] (its SBVR-style statement plus what enforces it) and a [Source]. Every core type carries a [Definition], most carry an [Example], and the ones whose shape comes straight from the case carry a [Source] with the URL it was taken from.

Four modelling decisions worth knowing

  1. Renter and driver are separated. Person is the supertype; Customer and Driver are subtypes. A company may rent a car it never drives, and a driver may drive on someone else's contract. A totality constraint spanning both subtype roles says every person must be at least one of the two.
  2. The tariff hangs off Car Group and Country — never off Car or Car Model. That is what makes "all cars in a car group have the same tariff" true by construction rather than by rule (R17).
  3. Life cycles are state-machine value constraints. Rental Status and Condition each carry their legal transitions, so what may follow what is visible in one place instead of being scattered over date facts.
  4. Four rental subtypes are derived, not asserted. One-Way, Advance, Walk-in and Open Rental follow from comparing branches, dates or state, so they carry no facts of their own.

Value constraints

Car Group Code — a labelled enumeration, in ascending tariff order. The order is what makes "a higher car group" meaningful in R12 and R13.

A=Economy   B=Compact   C=Intermediate   D=Full Size   E=Premium   F=Luxury

Condition — a state machine. A car enters the fleet Available, can only be rented from Available, and once written off never returns to service.

[*] -> Available
Available   -> Rented ; In Service ; Written Off
Rented      -> Available ; In Service ; Written Off
In Service  -> Available ; Written Off
Written Off -> [*]

Rental Status — a state machine. An advance booking is Requested, becomes Open at pick-up and Closed at return; it may be Cancelled before or during the rental, which is what R11, the remedial rule, needs.

[*] -> Requested
Requested -> Open ; Cancelled
Open      -> Closed ; Cancelled
Closed    -> [*]
Cancelled -> [*]

EU-Rent — business rules

The twenty rules held as rule OFTypes in the container EU-Rent Business Rules. Each rule in the repository carries its statement in a [Definition] annotation and its provenance in a [Source] annotation; both are reproduced here.

Structural means the model already enforces the rule through a uniqueness, totality or value constraint — no code can violate it. Derivation means the rule cannot be a constraint on a single fact type; since 2026-09-05 each such rule carries a [Derivation] annotation in the repository that states it as a check over role paths (for example FOR EACH Rental R: COUNT(Additional Driver[R]) <= 3), names the counter-example in the population that violates it, and says which threshold is assumed. The four derived Rental subtypes (Advance, Walk-in, One-Way, Open) are flagged derivable with their defining condition. The notation is readable, not executable — choosing a formal target is on the todo list. This is the distinction SBVR draws between structural (definitional) rules and behavioural rules, and it is the honest way to read the model: half of EU-Rent's rules are simply not expressible as constraints on single fact types.

Quoted verbatim

Two rules are quoted word for word from the Business Rules Journal, where they are used to show the SBVR distinction between a behavioural rule and the remedial rule that responds when it is broken:

R10 — "No rented car of an open rental may be outside the authorized area of the rental."

R11 — "An open rental must be canceled if the Area of Usage Rule is violated by the rental."

— SBVR Speaks on Rules: Violations of Business Rules, https://www.brcommunity.com/articles.php?id=c009

R03 is likewise a standard SBVR EU-Rent example statement: "It is obligatory that each rental has at most three additional drivers."

Where the model goes beyond the sources

Three thresholds are assumed, because the published material states the shape of the rule without a number: the minimum driver age of 21 (R06), the 90-day maximum rental period (R07), and the three bad experiences that bar a driver (R19). Each says so in its own annotation and each is a question on the project todo list.

Two structures are modelling choices, not statements of the case: the Person supertype with Customer and Driver subtypes (the sources treat renter and driver as separate roles but do not say how to type them), and the rental life cycle as a state machine (the sources speak of open, cancelled and closed rentals without giving a transition diagram).

The Business Rules Group's own EU-Rent material and the OMG SBVR annex contain considerably more rules than the twenty modelled here — the annex runs to roughly a hundred. The twenty were chosen to cover each kind of rule once: structural, behavioural, remedial, derivation, cardinality, and a join constraint.

Definition

R02 Rental Requests Exactly One Car Group

Definition
It is obligatory that each rental has exactly one requested car group. Enforced structurally: UC + TC on role 1 of "Requested Car Group".
Source
OMG SBVR EU-Rent vocabulary and rules (Annex). Summarised at https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/EU-Rent.html

R03 Maximum Three Additional Drivers

Definition
It is obligatory that each rental has at most three additional drivers. NOT expressible as a uniqueness or totality constraint: it is a cardinality rule over the "Additional Driver" fact type and needs a derivation/constraint expression or application logic.
Source
Classic SBVR EU-Rent example rule: "It is obligatory that each rental has at most three additional drivers." See https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/sbvr_intro_2.html
Derivation
FOR EACH Rental R: COUNT(Additional Driver WHERE Additional Driver.Rental = R) <= 3. A cardinality rule over the M:N fact type "Additional Driver"; it cannot be expressed as a uniqueness constraint, which bounds a count at 1, not at 3. Check on every insert into Additional Driver. Counter-example in the population: rental 10005 with four additional drivers.

R04 Barred Driver May Not Drive

Definition
It is prohibited that a barred driver is the main driver or an additional driver of a rental. A behavioural rule: it can be violated in the real world, which is exactly why EU-Rent records it.
Source
EU-Rent business context: "EU-Rent notes bad experiences with drivers and may bar drivers who cause them." https://caminao.blog/community/sample-problems/eu-rent-car/
Derivation
FOR EACH Rental R, FOR EACH Driver D IN {Main Driver of Rental[R].Driver} UNION Additional Driver[R].Driver: NOT EXISTS Barred Driver[D]. In other words the population of "Barred Driver" and the union of the two driver roles of a rental must be disjoint. A subset-exclusion constraint between "Barred Driver.Driver" and the driver roles of "Main Driver of Rental" and "Additional Driver" says the same thing declaratively. Counter-example in the population: rental 10004 driven by 1002, who is barred.

R05 Driver Holds Valid Licence

Definition
It is obligatory that each driver of a rental holds a driving licence whose expiry date is not earlier than the return date of the rental. Partly structural (TC makes licence and expiry mandatory for a Driver); the date comparison is a derivation rule.
Source
EU-Rent driver-qualification rules, OMG SBVR EU-Rent case study. https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/EU-Rent.html
Derivation
FOR EACH Rental R, FOR EACH Driver D IN {Main Driver of Rental[R].Driver} UNION Additional Driver[R].Driver: Licence Expiry[D].Licence Expiry Date >= Rental Period[R].Return Date, and >= Actual Return[R].Actual Return Date once the car is back. Existence of the licence and its expiry date is structural (TC on the Driver roles of "Driving Licence" and "Licence Expiry"); the date comparison is what this derivation adds. R20 Renter Drives Or Nominates A Driver

Definition
It is obligatory that the main driver of a rental is the renter of that rental, or that the renter has nominated the main driver. A renter need not drive (a company may rent), but somebody qualified must - hence Main Driver of Rental is mandatory for a Rental.
Source
EU-Rent: "each rented car has one or more registered drivers"; renter and driver are separate roles of a person in the EU-Rent vocabulary. ORM retelling of EU-Rent; https://kdmanalytics.com/sbvr/EU-Rent.html

Walk-in Rental

Definition
A rental contracted at the branch counter on the pick-up date itself, with no prior booking. Subtype of Rental. It goes straight to state Open, skipping the Requested state.
Derivation
Walk-in Rental := Rental R WHERE Rental State[R] entered 'Open' without ever having been 'Requested' - i.e. the rental was contracted on Rental Period[R].Pick Up Date itself. Needs the state history (transaction time on Rental State) to be evaluated exactly; approximated as NOT Advance Rental at the moment of contracting.
